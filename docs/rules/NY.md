# New York security deposit rules spec (as of 2026-10-03)

Secondary sources (Attorney General, Department of State, Homes and Community Renewal) were used only to cross-check readings, never as the citation.

Verified 2026-10-03 by a second agent, adversarially, against the same primary pages re-opened the same day plus the enacted chapter texts (HSTPA Part M sections 10, 25 and 29; chapters 428 and 789 of 2021; chapters 93 and 111 of 2022; chapters 431 and 436 of 2025). A second adversarial pass later the same day re-opened every one of those sources again and added RPL 235-g, NYC Civil Court Act 1801, the Court of Appeals' Kingston opinion and the 2026 FARE Act appellate decision; it found the Kingston adoption date wrong, the FARE Act litigation stale, the change ch. 436 made to successor liability unstated, one missed state rule (RPL 235-g) and two missed pending bills. This is the rulebook the app would enforce for a New York tenancy. The product never gives legal advice; it enforces, warns, informs and drafts for the landlord to approve (section L).

**Status of the law.** New York's residential deposit rules sit in General Obligations Law (GOL) article 7, title 1 [S6]. Four sections matter:

- **GOL 7-103** (trust, bank notice, interest for buildings with six or more units, the 1 percent administration fee). Unchanged; the nysenate.gov page shows no revision since 2014-09-22 [S3].
- **GOL 7-105** (sale or transfer of the building). Unchanged [S4].
- **GOL 7-108** (every dwelling unit not covered by 7-107): the cap, the move-in and pre-move-out inspections, the 14-day statement and refund, forfeiture, burden of proof, damages. Rewritten by the Housing Stability and Tenant Protection Act of 2019 (HSTPA), L.2019 ch. 36 (S6458/A8281), Part M section 25, which amended the heading and subdivision 1 (before it, 7-108 reached only units "with written leases in residential premises containing six or more dwelling units" and rent controlled units; since, "all dwelling units in residential premises") and added subdivision 1-a. Section 29 of Part M made section 25 effective "on the thirtieth day after this act shall have become a law" (signed June 14, 2019, so **July 14, 2019**) and applied it to "any lease or rental agreement or renewal of a lease or rental agreement entered into on or after such date" [S7]. Amended since by L.2021 ch. 428 (A4587-A, signed Sept 20, 2021, effective immediately: the seasonal-use exception, subdivisions 4 and 5; it also struck the words "under such contract" from paragraph (a), which now reads only "one month's rent") [S8]; L.2021 ch. 789 (S5105-C, signed Dec 22, 2021, effective immediately and applying to "actions and proceedings commenced on or after such effective date": the owner-occupied co-op exception, first numbered subdivision 4; drafted off the pre-428 text, its own version of paragraph (a) still read "one month's rent under such contract") [S9]; L.2022 ch. 93 (S7735, signed Feb 24, 2022, effective "on the same date and in the same manner as" ch. 789: reconciled the two 2021 versions of paragraph (a) without "under such contract", merged the two exceptions into it, renumbered the co-op subdivision to 6, excluded purchase-price payments, and let supervised co-ops charge approved screening fees under RPL 238-a(1)(b)) [S10]; and L.2022 ch. 111 (S7795, signed Feb 24, 2022, effective as ch. 428: the seasonal registry must be local or county, "or state" struck) [S11]. Last amended **2022-02-24**; nysenate.gov revision 2022-03-04 [S1].
- **GOL 7-107** (rent stabilized units: NYC Rent Stabilization Law of 1969 or the Emergency Tenant Protection Act of 1974). Until 2025 this section (added by ch. 917 of 1984) held only a successor-liability rule and a waiver clause: the grantee or assignee of a rent stabilized unit was liable for the tenant's deposit plus interest if its predecessor was, "whether or not the successor in interest has, upon the conveyance of such dwelling unit, received the sum as deposited" (old 7-107(2)(a), quoted in the bracketed text of S952-B [S12]). 7-108(1) excludes units "specifically referred to in section 7-107", so the HSTPA deposit rules did not reach rent stabilized tenancies through the GOL. L.2025 ch. 436 (S952-B/A6423-A), signed **Oct 16, 2025**, rewrote 7-107 with the same cap, inspections, 14-day rule, forfeiture, burden and damages, and replaced the strict successor rule with the actual-knowledge regime copied from 7-108(2) (now 7-107(9)), effective on the thirtieth day, **Nov 15, 2025**, for "any lease or rental agreement or renewal ... entered into on or after such date" [S2][S12]. HCR Fact Sheet 9 (11/2025) confirms the date [S19].

Adjacent state rules the engine needs: Real Property Law (RPL) 238-a (no fees before or at the start of a tenancy except a background and credit check fee capped at $20; late fees only after five days and capped at $50 or 5 percent) [S14], RPL 235-g (an electronic billing or payment system may not be the only way to pay rent, and a tenant who declines one may not be charged for it) [S31], and General Construction Law (GCL) 20 and 25-a (how to count the 14 days) [S15].

**Pending, not law** (as of 2026-10-03): S4856/A2652 (2025) would stretch the 14 days to 30; both referred to Judiciary, last action Jan 7, 2026 [S13]. Also in committee with the same last action: A6696 (would amend 7-103(2) on deposit accounts), S3164 (7-103, deposits payable in installments), A629 (7-103, a tenancy deposit protection program), A8078 and A4355 (each a new GOL 7-104 on the return of deposits) and S6397/A1431 (a new RPL 238-b requiring deposit alternatives such as six installments). No bill touching 7-103, 7-107 or 7-108 was found enacted after ch. 436 of 2025, and the nysenate.gov pages for 7-103 and 7-108 still show their 2014 and 2022 revisions.

**What New York does not have**, so the engine must not carry these over from the California spec: no statutory photo duty, no documentation threshold, no required delivery method for the statement, no electronic-refund mandate, no multi-tenant payee rule, no statutory definition of wear and tear, and no requirement to attach receipts. Instead it has a hard 14-day forfeiture, the landlord's burden of proof, and a trust-and-interest regime for the money while it is held.

**Why this matters for PayPal:** the statute says only that the landlord "shall return any remaining portion of the deposit to the tenant" within 14 days, so a PayPal refund of the original payment is a lawful way to return it and needs no statutory agreement (rule N27). The constraint sits earlier: under 7-103(1) the deposit "shall be held in trust" and "shall not be mingled with the personal moneys" of the landlord. A deposit paid by PayPal invoice lands in the landlord's PayPal balance next to rent, and for a building with six or more units it must be in an interest-bearing account at a New York bank (7-103(2-a)). So the app must tell the landlord to move the money out and record where it went, and must account for interest at close-out (rules N2 to N6).

## B. What the app must collect at move-in

1. **Unit facts.** Regulation status (unregulated, rent stabilized, rent controlled, other carve-out), the building's dwelling-unit count (six or more triggers N4), city and county (section I overlays), and whether the unit is a registered seasonal use unit or an owner-occupied co-op (N8, N9). 7-108(1), (1-a), (4)-(6); 7-103(2-a).
2. **Lease facts.** Signing date, occupancy start, term, monthly rent, every amount asked before or at the start (deposit, advance rent, fees), the utility clause, the late-fee clause, and a scan for waivers and "nonrefundable" wording (N7, N10 to N13). 7-108(1-a)(a)-(b), (3); RPL 238-a.
3. **The lawful deposit.** One month's rent, less nothing, plus nothing (N7). The PayPal invoice is issued for that amount only, and its record is the receipt. The invoice is offered next to at least one non-electronic way to pay, with no fee for choosing it (N32).
4. **Custody of the money.** Where the deposit will be held (a separate account; for six or more units an interest-bearing New York bank account at the prevailing rate), the bank's name and address, the rate, the date the money left PayPal, and the written bank notice to the tenant (N2 to N4). 7-103(1), (2), (2-a). The notice goes out as soon as the account is recorded.
5. **The move-in inspection offer.** Date and method of the offer, the tenant's answer, and, if requested, the signed condition agreement dated before occupancy with every noted defect itemized by room (N14, N15). 7-108(1-a)(c). The app blocks the tenancy from starting without the offer on record, and without the agreement when the tenant asked.
6. **Move-in photos.** Not required by statute. Recommended, because the landlord carries the burden of proof (N22) and because the condition agreement covers only what the tenant asked to note.
7. **Tenant contacts and designations.** Email and a forwarding address when known (N28); a written designation of the refund destination when it will not be the original PayPal payment (N27); the allocation agreement for several adults (N29); the tenant's choice for interest (annual payment, held in trust, or applied to rent) when an interest-bearing account is used (N5).

## C. The deadline and what starts the clock

- **14 days**, not 21. Both the itemized statement and the remaining money must be with the tenant inside them. 7-108(1-a)(e); 7-107(6).
- **The trigger is vacating:** "within fourteen days after the tenant has vacated the premises". The statute does not define "vacated". The app uses the earliest defensible date: the day the tenant gave up possession (keys returned, belongings out), not the lease end date and not the date the landlord inspected. When the tenant leaves before the lease ends, the clock still starts at vacating. When the tenant holds over, it has not started.
- **Counting:** GCL 20 excludes the day of the event, so vacate day is day 0 and the deadline is day 14. GCL 25-a extends a period ending on a Saturday, Sunday or public holiday to the next business day, and the DOS reads 7-108 that way (FAQ 3). The app shows the extension but targets day 14 (N20).
- **Counted in New York time (Andrew's decision of 2026-10-05).** Day 14, today, and the day the statement and the refund went are calendar days in America/New_York, wherever the landlord is. A refund sent at 9:30 PM Pacific on day 14 went at 12:30 AM on day 15 in New York, and is late; one sent at 8:30 PM Pacific (11:30 PM Eastern) is on time. At 9:30 PM Pacific a statement handed over that New York day can be recorded with it, and the close-out, the next step and Properties already count the next day. Daylight saving is the zone's own: 11:30 PM EST on the night clocks fall back is still that day. `scripts/zone-check.mjs` checks both edges against day 14.
- **Missing it forfeits everything** the landlord wanted to keep (N21). There is no cure period and no good-faith exception in the text.
- **The app's rule (Andrew's decision of 2026-10-04): a hard block.** Until 2026-10-04 the app held the deductions after day 14 but let the landlord keep them by recording a reason (the "late-deduction acknowledgment", action W). That override is gone. The rule (`nyLate` in `src/lib/states/ny.ts`, the same for every part of the app) and its cases on both sides of the deadline, each checked by `scripts/state-packs-check.mjs` or `scripts/lawful-range-check.mjs`:
  - The last day is day 14 after the vacate date, or the next business day when day 14 is a Saturday, Sunday or GCL 24 public holiday. Out 2026-09-26, a Saturday day 14 (2026-10-10) before Columbus Day: a statement on Tuesday 2026-10-13 is on time, one on Wednesday 2026-10-14 is late.
  - The statement delivered on day 14 is on time; on day 15 it is late, whatever the refund.
  - The statement on time and part of the deposit owed back: on time if the refund goes by the last day; late once the last day is behind with the refund unsent, or sent after it.
  - The refund made in PayPal rather than from here, and recorded as the deposit's refund (`src/lib/reconcile.ts`, `outsideReturnedAt`): the day the rest of the deposit went is the day those refunds, with any PayPal dispute's money, first added up to what the deductions leave owed. One on day 5 with the statement on day 6 is on time; $1,000 on day 5 and the last $500 on day 19 is late. The close-out, the statement, the next step, Properties, Payments, the tenants' page and a dispute's lawful ranges all read that day (`scripts/reconcile-check.mjs`).
  - The statement on time and nothing left to refund (the deductions use the whole deposit): on time, before and after day 14.
  - No deductions: nothing to forfeit, so no block.
  - A refund already sent (on time or not): the close-out can't send another, so it doesn't block; a late one is still printed as forfeited on the statement, with any part of the deposit not yet returned as "Still owed", and gives the tenants a $0 lawful range in a dispute. The unit stays open while anything is still owed: the next step is "Day 14 has passed: $X more owed" (overdue, to the statement), Properties and Payments count the rest as held and due back, and Move-outs doesn't list it as closed (`forfeitShort` in `src/lib/next-step.ts`). A dispute's settlement can send the rest: then the unit closes, and the ledger counts the settlement as money toward the whole deposit, never as a deduction given back (`settledOff` in `src/lib/ledger.ts`). Otherwise the rest goes from the PayPal account, outside Good Faith, since the close-out sends one refund per tenancy.
  - Late: the close-out refuses any refund until every deduction is removed, and then refunds the whole deposit; the next step is "Day 14 has passed: refund in full", before the statement goes out and after, and its button, "Remove deductions", opens the Move-out tab's Deductions card, which offers "Remove all deductions" (one confirmation) only while that is the step; the statement, its PDF and the tenant page keep the deduction lines as what was claimed, print "Forfeited after day 14" with this section's citation, add the deductions back in the totals and show the whole deposit as "Owed back"; a dispute's lawful range is $0 on every line, rent too, and the landlord's negotiator concedes every charge in code, citing 7-108(1-a)(e), with no model call (New York only: its statute names no defense, `late.noDefense`).
  - Forfeited deductions are not deducted: the Payments ledger marks them "Forfeited after day 14" and counts them as neither deducted nor kept (the deposit stays held until it all goes back), Properties' "Deducted this year" and Insights leave them out, and the unit summary leaves the whole deposit to return (`deadline.forfeits`, the same rule). The close-out's refund of the whole deposit carries the note "Your whole security deposit, $X, is returned: nothing is kept from it", in place of "less itemized deductions" (`plan.noteToPayer` in `closeout.ts`, sent by code whatever the agent wrote).
  - While a dispute the tenants sent is open, negotiating or agreed, its deductions are frozen and can't be removed, so the close-out returns the whole deposit with them still on file (`wholeDepositOwed` in `src/lib/refund-rail.ts`; a warning on the refund page says so) and the next step is that refund. The dispute's settlement then has no money left to move: it records what was agreed, and the amended statement shows nothing more sent. The close-out never sends less than the whole deposit after day 14.
- **Rent stabilized leases entered before 2025-11-15** are not under 7-107(6) until renewal. The app still runs the 14-day flow for them, marked as the app's practice rather than the statute's command (section M).

## D. How the statement must be delivered

The statute requires an "itemized statement indicating the basis for the amount of the deposit retained" and nothing about its form or route. So:

- In writing, dated, with the vacate date and the day-14 date shown.
- Sent by a method that leaves a dated record: first-class mail to the forwarding address the tenant gave, plus email when the tenant gave one. With no forwarding address and no email, mail to the vacated unit and flag the statement as possibly undelivered (N28).
- No statutory consent is needed for email, but an email alone to an address the tenant never confirmed is weak proof that the landlord "provided" the statement; the app asks for the tenant's email at move-in and in the moving-out form.
- The pre-move-out itemized statement (N18) follows the same rule.

## E. Documentation rules

- **No statutory attachment duty and no dollar threshold.** New York does not require receipts, invoices or photos with the statement.
- **The burden of proof is the landlord's** (N22), so the app treats evidence as required in practice: a vendor invoice or receipt for each repair, a description, hours and rate for the landlord's own labor, before and after photos for repairs and cleaning, and the move-in record (condition agreement, photos). A deduction without evidence is flagged "Unsupported" on the statement draft, never blocked.
- **The two statutory writings are evidence only in deposit proceedings:** the move-in condition agreement (N14) and the post-inspection statement (N18). Keep both with the file.
- **Interest accounting** (N5, N6) is part of the statement when any interest-bearing account was used.
- **Estimates.** The statute says nothing about estimates. A deduction based on an estimate is still a "reasonable and itemized" cost the landlord must prove; the app marks it as an estimate and asks for the final invoice when it arrives.

## F. Penalties

- **Forfeiture:** no statement and refund within 14 days, no right to retain any portion (N21). 7-108(1-a)(e); 7-107(6).
- **Actual damages** for any violation of the subdivision; **punitive damages up to twice the deposit or advance** for a willful violation (N23). 7-108(1-a)(g); 7-107(8).
- **Burden of proof** on the landlord in any dispute (N22). 7-108(1-a)(f); 7-107(7).
- **Commingling:** 7-103 carries no damages clause, but New York courts treat a commingled deposit as converted, with the tenant entitled to its immediate return regardless of the landlord's claims (LeRoy v. Sayers, 217 A.D.2d 63 (1st Dept 1995), as applied in later Civil Court decisions) [S23]. Case law, not statute: the app states it as a risk, not a rule.
- **Sale without turning over and notifying:** a misdemeanor (N24). 7-105(3).
- **Attorney General** actions to compel compliance, plus costs up to $2,000 per respondent (N30). 7-109.
- **Fees:** charging a barred move-in fee or an over-cap late fee violates RPL 238-a; a lease clause "waiving or limiting" that section is "void as against public policy" (238-a(3)), and a clause waiving RPL 235-g is "void as contrary to public policy" (235-g(2)).
- **Forum:** small claims (NYC Civil Court up to $10,000 "exclusive of interest and costs", NYC Civil Court Act 1801, which also lets a tenant sue there when the rented property is in the city [S30]; city courts outside the city $5,000; town and village courts $3,000, per the courts' self-help page [S28]) or a complaint to the Attorney General's Consumer Frauds and Protection Bureau, per the AG and HCR guidance [S16][S19].

## G. How the refund may be paid

- **The statute is silent on method.** "Return any remaining portion of the deposit to the tenant" within 14 days is the whole rule. No electronic mandate, no check requirement, no designated-account rule, no multi-tenant payee rule.
- **PayPal refund** of the original invoice payment: allowed, no separate agreement needed, because the money goes back where it came from. The app's refund rail stays as it is for California.
- **PayPal Payout** to another destination: allowed, but only to an account the tenant designated in writing (N27). Without a designation the app does not pay out; it asks the tenant through the private link.
- **Interest:** when an interest-bearing account was used, the tenant's share of interest less the 1 percent fee is returned with the deposit (N5, N6), itemized on the statement.
- **Several adult tenants:** one refund to the original payment, or a split under a written allocation signed by all adults (N29).
- **Timing:** the refund must be sent so that it is with the tenant by day 14; the app records PayPal's id and timestamp as the proof.
- **While the money is held:** not in the PayPal balance. See N2 to N4; the app warns at the moment the invoice is paid.

## H. Wear and tear

The statute uses "normal wear and tear" and "ordinary wear and tear of occupancy" and never defines either; it also bars any charge for "damage caused by a prior tenant". 7-108(1-a)(b); 7-107(3). New York publishes no state guide with examples or a useful-life method. The Attorney General's guide says only that the landlord may use the deposit for "the reasonable cost of repairs beyond normal wear and tear" [S16].

So the app reuses the item table and proration method of the California spec (docs/LEGAL_RULES.md, section C) for New York tenancies, labeled **Common practice (L)** throughout, never attributed to New York law or to any New York agency. Two statutory anchors are confidence H: wear and tear is never deductible, and a prior tenant's damage is never deductible. Everything else in that table is the app's practice, and the copy says so.

## J. Itemized statement checklist

1. Tenant name(s), unit address, vacate date, statement date; the day-14 date shown. 7-108(1-a)(e)
2. Deposit received: amount, date, how paid (the PayPal invoice), where it was held, and the bank notice date when a bank account was used. 7-103(1), (2)
3. Interest: rate, period, interest earned, the 1 percent fee, interest already paid, interest returned now. 7-103(2), (2-a), (2-b)
4. One line per deduction, each tagged (1) rent, (2) damage beyond wear and tear, (3) utilities payable to the landlord under the lease, or (4) moving and storage; nothing for wear and tear, prior tenants, late fees or other charges. 7-108(1-a)(b)
5. Nothing that appears on the move-in condition agreement. 7-108(1-a)(c)
6. The pre-move-out inspection: date, the items listed, the items the tenant cured; any final item not on that list marked as arising after the inspection or hidden at the time. 7-108(1-a)(d)
7. Evidence for each deduction (invoice or receipt, labor description with hours and rate, photos), marked "Unsupported" where missing. 7-108(1-a)(f)
8. Estimates marked as estimates, with the vendor and the expected final invoice date.
9. Refund: amount, method (PayPal refund of the original payment, or Payout to the designated account), destination, date sent, PayPal id. 7-108(1-a)(e)
10. If deductions exceed the deposit: the balance stated as a claim, not a demand for immediate payment.
11. Delivery record: method, address or email, date. 7-108(1-a)(e)
12. For rent stabilized units: the 7-107 citations in place of 7-108, and the RSC 2525.4 interest election.
13. The disclaimer line (section L).

## K. Edge cases

- **Tenant gives less than two weeks' notice.** The landlord's duty to send the inspection-rights notice falls away (N16); the inspection window may be impossible. Record the notice date and skip the draft with an explanation. The 14-day clock is unaffected.
- **Landlord gives the termination notice.** The rights notice is always owed, "within a reasonable time" after it.
- **Deposit over the cap.** Collected under a lease signed on or after 2019-07-14 (2025-11-15 for rent stabilized): block at intake; if already collected, the excess is refundable at once, since the clause is void under 7-108(3). Collected under an older lease: W, with the drop to one month due at renewal (N26).
- **Prepaid last month's rent.** Counts toward the cap (N7). An existing prepayment above the cap is handled like an over-cap deposit.
- **Holding over.** The tenant has not "vacated"; the clock has not started. Rent for the holdover days is a category (1) deduction once they leave.
- **Partial month.** Unpaid prorated rent for days occupied is category (1). Rent for days after vacating is not "non-payment of rent" and is not deducted; the app flags lease-break claims for the landlord to pursue outside the deposit.
- **Abandoned belongings.** Category (4) covers moving and storage costs; the app itemizes them with receipts. Disposal and sale of the property itself are outside this tool.
- **Deductions exceed the deposit.** The statement shows the balance claimed; the tool drafts only, and the burden of reasonableness stays with the landlord.
- **Building sold mid-tenancy.** The 7-105 workflow (N24): turn over within five days, registered or certified mail to the tenant, record the mailing. The buyer's 30-day notice (N25) is their task; for a rent stabilized unit on a lease from before 2025-11-15 the buyer should assume the old strict successor rule (N25).
- **Six or more units but the deposit sat in PayPal.** 7-103(2-a) was not met while it sat there. The app cannot cure that; it records the date the money moved and the interest from that date, and shows the gap.
- **Rent stabilized lease signed before 2025-11-15.** 7-107's deposit rules start at the next renewal. The app runs the same flow and marks each 7-107 citation "from renewal"; RSC 2525.4 (cap and interest) applies throughout.
- **Seasonal rental without a registry.** No exception: the one-month cap applies even to a 90-day summer lease.
- **Co-op shareholder.** Out of scope (N9): the product is for landlords of rental units.
- **Day 14 on a weekend or holiday.** GCL 25-a extends to the next business day; the app still shows day 14 as the target and the extension as information.

## M. Open questions

1. **"Deposit or advance" and the first month's rent.** The cap text does not say that first month's rent paid at signing is excluded, or that deposit and advance are summed. The AG and DOS read it as one month in total on top of the first month's rent, and the engine follows that reading. A court could read it per item.
2. **"Vacated".** Undefined. Keys returned, last belongings out, or lease end; the app uses the earliest. Confirm with counsel whether a tenant who leaves belongings behind has "vacated".
3. **Rent stabilized leases from before 2025-11-15.** Whether a tenant on such a lease can invoke 7-107's new rules before renewal (the chapter applies to leases and renewals "entered into on or after" the effective date). The app applies the 14-day flow regardless and labels it.
4. **Statement delivery and proof.** With no statutory method, what proof of "provided" a court will accept. Mail plus email with a dated record is the app's practice.
5. **Refund destination.** Whether a PayPal refund that PayPal cannot complete (closed tenant account) counts as returned on the day it was sent. The app treats a failed refund as not returned and re-routes to a designated Payout.
6. **Interest rate.** "Prevailing rate" has no published figure; the app uses the account's actual rate and shows it.
7. **Cleaning.** Not one of the four categories by name; the app treats it as category (2) only when it restores a condition beyond wear and tear. Confirm.
8. **Estimates and late invoices.** No statutory estimate or 14-day follow-up rule as in California; the app's estimate flag is practice.
9. **Second revision of 7-107 (2025-11-21).** The nysenate.gov page lists revisions dated 2025-10-17 and 2025-11-21; the current text matches S952-B as signed and no chapter amendment to ch. 436 was found. Likely the effective-date processing; re-check when the 2026 session's chapters are published.
10. **RPL 238-a(2-a).** Resolved: A56-B (ch. 431 of 2025) section 3 reads "This act shall take effect immediately and shall apply to actions and proceedings commenced on and after such effective date", so Oct 16, 2025. The cap is the greater of the landlord's actual bank cost or the $20 in GOL 5-328(3), only if the lease provides for the fee. Marginal here, since such fees are not deductible anyway (N13).
11. **Judiciary Law 478, 484 and 495 and Executive Law 296(5).** Resolved: re-fetched from nysenate.gov on 2026-10-03. The 296 page carries a 2026-06-05 revision (the housing subdivision 5(a) reads as cited); 478, 484 and 495 are unchanged on point.
12. **Pending bills.** S4856/A2652 (2025) would make the deadline 30 days; A6696, A629 and A8078 would touch 7-103 or add a 7-104; S6397/A1431 would require deposit alternatives. All were in committee as of Jan 7, 2026. Watch the 2026 session; if S4856 is enacted, N19 to N21 and the pack change.
13. **ETPA cities.** Newburgh's and Poughkeepsie's adoptions were voided at the trial level per HCR; Poughkeepsie was reported to be appealing in May 2025, and neither an appellate decision nor a new vacancy study for either city was found as of 2026-10-03. Nyack opted out. Kingston is settled (Court of Appeals, June 18, 2025; opt-in effective Aug 1, 2022).
14. **RSC 2525.4 grandfather clause.** The code lets a pre-stabilization deposit above one month continue "during the term of such lease and any renewals thereof with the same tenant"; HCR Fact Sheet 9 says HSTPA requires a refund of the excess. The app follows HCR and flags the conflict; confirm with counsel for a unit that came under stabilization with a two-month deposit already held.
15. **Successor liability for rent stabilized leases from before 2025-11-15.** Old 7-107(2)(a) made a buyer liable for the deposit whether or not it received the money; ch. 436 applies the actual-knowledge rule only to leases and renewals entered on or after its effective date. Which rule a court applies to a sale after Nov 15, 2025 of a unit still on an older lease is untested; the app flags it (N25, section K).
16. **RPL 235-g and the deposit.** The section bars an electronic-only method and any surcharge "for the payment of rent"; a deposit is not rent in ordinary usage, and no decision applying 235-g to deposits was found. The app follows it for the deposit anyway (N32), since the same invoice flow collects rent. RPL 235-e (a written receipt for rent paid in cash or by an instrument other than the tenant's personal check, within 15 days when paid indirectly) is likewise written for rent; the PayPal invoice record serves as the receipt in practice.
