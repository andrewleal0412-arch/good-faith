**Status of the law.** The deposit sections, RCW 59.18.260 to 59.18.285, were last amended by SHB 1074 (2023 c 331), effective July 23, 2023 [S14][S36]. That act moved the deadline from 21 to 30 days, required estimates or invoices with the statement, replaced the checklist's older item list (walls, floors, countertops, carpets, drapes, furniture, appliances) with four named groups and moved the free-replacement-copy sentence from subsection (4) to (3), made the landlord liable for the deposit when no checklist was given, barred carpet-cleaning and undocumented-item charges, added the rent-and-other-charges exception in 59.18.280(4), and defined "wear resulting from ordinary use of the premises" in 59.18.030 [S1][S3][S6]. Its section 1 is findings and intent (not an RCW amendment); sections 2 to 4 are the deposit sections (59.18.030, .260, .280); sections 5 to 7 replaced "normal wear and tear" in 59.18.060 and 59.18.130 and amended the deceased-tenant timelines in 59.18.595 [S36]. Earlier steps: ESB 6413 (2016 c 66, effective June 9, 2016) moved the deadline from 14 to 21 days (its section 4 strikes "fourteen" for "twenty-one" in both places) [S15]; SB 5600 (2019 c 356, effective July 28, 2019) added 59.18.283 on applying payments to rent first [S4][S16]; EHB 1694 (2020 c 169, effective June 11, 2020) added installments in 59.18.610 and the 25% cap on holding deposits [S7][S8][S17]; HB 2535 (2020 c 177, effective June 11, 2020) added the five-day late-fee rule to 59.18.170 and 59.18.230 [S13]; ESHB 2064 (2022 c 81, effective June 9, 2022) added 59.18.670 on fees in lieu of a deposit [S9]; HB 1593 (2022 c 196, effective June 9, 2022) added the landlord mitigation program bar in 59.18.280(3)(a) [S3][S18]; SSB 5749 (2022 c 95, effective June 9, 2022) added 59.18.230(2)(j), the bar on electronic-only rent payment clauses, and the payment-method rule in 59.18.063(1) [S44].

- 2026: 2026 c 55 (SSB 5937, smart access systems, effective January 1, 2027) renumbers the definitions in 59.18.030 without changing the wear definition, and adds 59.18.750 to 59.18.760 on smart access systems; the wear definition becomes (45) and "tenant representative" becomes (40) [S6]. 2026 c 184 (ESSB 6200, portable cooling devices, effective June 11, 2026 per the Legislature's bill summary page) adds 59.18.740, whose subsection (11) says nothing in that section precludes "retaining a portion of a security deposit for a lawful purpose under RCW 59.18.280": damage from a tenant's cooling device is an ordinary damage deduction with the usual documents, and no new deposit rule [S35]. 2026 c 234 (SSB 6237, effective June 11, 2026) amends 59.18.060 to add a flood-risk disclosure for leases made after December 31, 2026; nothing on deposits [S40].

Washington has **no statewide deposit cap** for chapter 59.18 tenancies (and no statewide pet deposit cap), **no deposit interest owed to the tenant** (the trust account's interest is the landlord's unless agreed otherwise), **no photo requirement** (the required record is the signed written checklist), and **no pre-move-out inspection right**. Local ordinances add caps and installment rules in Seattle, Tacoma, Bellingham and Olympia (section L).

**Why this matters for PayPal.** The statute's compliance safe harbor is the statement "together with the payment of any refund due" being "delivered to the tenant personally or deposited in the United States mail" to the tenant's last known address within 30 days (59.18.280(1)(a)) [S3]. It says nothing about the form of payment and nothing about electronic refunds. Two consequences for the app: (1) a PayPal refund is not forbidden, but it is outside the safe harbor, so the app records the tenant's written agreement to an electronic refund at move-in and still hands or mails the paper statement, with the PayPal refund id on it, inside the 30 days (rules W17, W18, section G); (2) the deposit itself must sit in a trust account at a Washington financial institution or licensed escrow agent during the tenancy (59.18.270) [S2]; a PayPal business balance is not one, so the app tells the landlord to move the invoice proceeds and records the depository for the receipt and notice (W6, W7).

## B. What the app must collect at move-in

1. The written lease, with the deposit clause and its withholding terms (W1), any damage clause, every fee with its label (W5; a nonrefundable cleaning fee switches off normal-cleaning deductions, W31), any late-fee clause (W29), and a scan for waiver, nondisclosure or electronic-only payment clauses (W16).
2. The move-in checklist: condition and cleanliness or existing damage, room by room, covering at least walls (paint and wallpaper), carpets and other flooring, furniture and appliances (W2). Photos attach to each line as evidence; they do not replace the written line.
3. Both signatures and dates on the checklist, and the record that the tenant received a copy (W3). An electronic signature is probably valid under chapter 1.80 RCW; see open question 3.
4. The trust account: institution, address and location (W6), the date the funds reached it, and the receipt and depository notice sent to the tenant (W7). With a PayPal invoice, the app asks for the trust account before it marks the receipt as sent.
5. The written interest agreement, if any (W8).
6. Installment facts: lease term, first full month's rent, deposit and fee totals, whether last month's rent is due up front, any written installment request, and the signed schedule (W10).
7. Any holding deposit and its written conditions, and where it is credited (W12).
8. Whether a fee in lieu of a deposit is in play (W13: out of scope, flag).
9. Tenancy type (month-to-month or fixed term) and the landlord's terms for the other type, for the parity check (W14).
10. The city, for the local overlays (W15, section L).
11. The tenant's written agreement to an electronic refund by PayPal, and the address the statement goes to (section G, W18).

## C. The deadline and what starts the clock

- 30 days (59.18.280(1)(a)) [S3]. The clock starts when both have happened: the rental agreement has terminated and the tenant has vacated. The app starts the countdown on the later of the two dates. A tenant who leaves early on a fixed term with the lease still running is the gray case (open question 1); the app shows the 30 days from the move-out and warns that the statute's words are "termination ... and vacation".
- Abandonment: when the tenant defaults in rent "and reasonably indicates by words or actions the intention not to resume tenancy" (59.18.310(1)), the 30 days run from the day the landlord learns of it [S12]. The app records that date and the evidence.
- The statute counts calendar days and names no extension; the only statutory excuses are circumstances beyond the landlord's control and abandonment (W25). RCW 1.12.040, the general computation-of-time statute, excludes the first day and, when the last day "is a holiday, Saturday, or Sunday", excludes that day too; the Reviser's note records that the courts treat it as of general application [S31]. So a day 30 that lands on a weekend or holiday arguably rolls to the next business day, but no decision applies it to 59.18.280. The app does not rely on it: it shows the 30th calendar day as the deadline and flags the weekend case as "mail by the Friday before".
- Deceased sole tenant (59.18.595): the statement and refund go to the "tenant representative" (defined in 59.18.030(35)) within "the same number of days as required under RCW 59.18.280" after that representative removes the tenant's property [S33]. The app records the representative, the proof they gave, and the removal date, and starts the 30 days there.
- There is no waiting period for a forwarding address: the statement goes to the "last known address", which may be the vacated unit (W18).

## D. How the statement must be delivered

- Personal delivery, or first-class mail "properly addressed to the tenant's last known address", deposited within the 30 days (59.18.280(1)(a)) [S3]. Mailing on day 30 complies.
- Email, text and portal messages are not in the safe harbor. The app sends them only as a courtesy copy and records the paper delivery as the one that counts.
- The refund goes with the statement ("together with the payment of any refund due"). A refund made another way (PayPal) is recorded on the statement with its id, date and amount so the paper record shows the money moved (section G).
- Multiple tenants: the statute is silent. The app addresses the statement to every adult tenant on the lease at the last known address each gave, in one envelope per address (open question 4).

## E. Documentation rules

- Attach to the statement: estimates received or invoices paid for each damage charge (59.18.280(1)(b)) [S3].
- Landlord's or employee's own work: the bill, invoice or receipt for materials or supplies; for stock on hand, "a bill, invoice, receipt, vendor price list, or other vendor document that reasonably documents the cost"; and a statement of the time spent and the reasonable hourly rate (W19).
- The move-in checklist is the baseline: an item's repair or replacement is chargeable only if its condition was "reasonably documented" there (W22). Photos help prove the checklist line; they do not substitute for it.
- Carpet cleaning needs documented wear beyond ordinary use (W21). Partial damage is priced as the damaged portion (W23).
- No dollar threshold excuses the documents, and there is no tenant waiver. Rent and other non-damage charges need no checklist or invoice (W24), only their line on the statement.
- A nonrefundable cleaning fee, once collected, bars any charge for normal cleaning (59.18.130(10), W31); only cleaning beyond normal remains chargeable, with its documents. Conditions caused by the landlord's own failure to meet a chapter 59.18 duty (an unrepaired leak that stained a floor) are outside the tenant's restoration duty and not chargeable.
- What the statute does not require: photos, a pre-move-out inspection, a written explanation of cost beyond the documents above. The app keeps its move-in and move-out photos as evidence for the checklist and the "reasonably substantiate" standard.

## F. Penalties

- No checklist at move-in: liable for the amount of the deposit, plus the prevailing party's costs and fees; this does not limit the tenant's 59.18.280 recovery (59.18.260(5)) [S1].
- Statement, documents or refund late: liable for the full deposit, and barred in the tenant's suit "from asserting any claim or raising any defense for retaining any of the deposit", unless circumstances beyond the landlord's control or abandonment (59.18.280(2)) [S3].
- Intentional refusal to give the statement, documents or refund: up to twice the deposit, at the court's discretion, same excuses (59.18.280(2)).
- Costs and a reasonable attorneys' fee to the prevailing party in any action to recover the deposit (59.18.280(2); 59.18.270).
- Nonrefundable fee without a written lease: liable for the fees collected; a fee the lease does not label nonrefundable is a refundable deposit (59.18.285) [S5].
- Refusing a written installment request: one month's rent plus fees (59.18.610(5)) [S8].
- Holding deposit rules broken: the fee or deposit, plus up to twice it (59.18.253(5)) [S7].
- Foreclosed owner who neither refunds nor transfers the deposit: up to twice the deposit (59.18.270) [S2].
- Prohibited lease clauses used knowingly: actual damages plus statutory damages up to two times the monthly rent, costs and fees (59.18.230(3)) [S11].
- Parity and rent-limit violations (59.18.700(5)): excess amounts, up to three months of the unlawful charges, fees; Attorney General penalties up to $7,500 per violation [S10].
- Fee in lieu of a deposit, material violation (59.18.670(5)): up to two times the monthly rent plus costs and fees [S9].
- Condemnation or no-occupancy order (59.18.085): the tenant recovers the entire deposit and all prepaid rent; when relocation assistance is owed under (3), the landlord pays the deposit and prepaid rent within seven days of the agency's notice, by certified check [S34]. Out of scope for the app; flag.
- Domestic violence and related terminations (59.18.575(2)(b)): the full deposit is returned notwithstanding a forfeiture clause, subject to the 59.18.280 process [S32].

## G. How the refund may be paid

- The statute names no payment instrument. The safe harbor is the refund delivered personally or mailed first class with the statement within 30 days (59.18.280(1)(a)) [S3]. A check in the envelope is the conservative path.
- Electronic refunds are neither authorized nor prohibited. There is no Washington counterpart to California's electronic-refund rule, no case found, and no agency guidance found (open question 2). The app's position: a PayPal refund of the original payment, or a Payout to the PayPal email the tenant designated, is allowed only when the tenant agreed in writing at move-in or later; the paper statement still goes out inside the 30 days and carries PayPal's refund id, date and amount. The agreement is the tenant's choice of how to receive money, not a waiver of a chapter right, so 59.18.230 should not be in play; that is the app's reading, not a holding.
- Where the money comes from: the deposit must be in the trust account during the tenancy (W6). The statute does not say the refund must be drawn from that account; the landlord may fund the PayPal refund from the business account and reconcile the trust account. The app records both legs.
- Interest: none to the tenant unless a written agreement says so (W8).
- Joint tenants: statute silent; one refund to the payment account the deposit came from unless every adult tenant signs an allocation (practice, as in Texas).
- Timing of a PayPal refund of the original payment: inside PayPal's 180-day window, otherwise a Payout. The 30-day statute clock, not PayPal's window, is the binding one.

## J. Edge cases

- **Tenant leaves before the lease ends.** "Termination ... and vacation" are both needed. If the landlord treats the lease as ended at move-out (re-lets, accepts the keys as surrender), the clock runs from the move-out; if the lease is held open for rent, the clock arguably runs from its end, but the app counts from the move-out and warns, because the shorter reading is the safe one.
- **Abandonment.** Rent default plus words or actions showing no intent to return (59.18.310(1)). The app records the day the landlord learned of it and starts the 30 days there; rent for the 59.18.310 period is a rent deduction, with the mitigation duty noted.
- **No checklist at move-in.** The deposit should not have been collected (W2) and the landlord owes its amount (W4). The app blocks every damage, cleaning and carpet deduction (W22), allows rent and other charges (W24), and tells the landlord a full refund of the deposit is the lawful close. It cannot be fixed after the fact; a checklist signed later is not "at the commencement".
- **Checklist signed by the landlord only.** 59.18.260(3) wants both signatures. The app treats an unsigned checklist as missing for W22 and records the tenant's refusal if that is what happened (open question 3).
- **Carpet.** Cleaning needs documented beyond-ordinary wear (W21); replacement needs the checklist line and the damaged-portion limit (W22, W23).
- **Deposit paid through PayPal.** The invoice proceeds land in the landlord's PayPal balance. The app asks for the trust account and the transfer date before it drafts the receipt and depository notice (W6, W7).
- **Installments outstanding at move-out.** The landlord "may treat" an unpaid deposit or fee installment as rent owing (59.18.283(3)); when the landlord does, it is a rent deduction (W24). The statute permits the treatment, it does not impose it.
- **Nonrefundable cleaning fee in the lease.** No deduction for normal cleaning (59.18.130(10), W31); a cleaning deduction needs the beyond-normal showing and its documents. Seattle's SMC 7.24.035.B.3 restates the same rule; it is state law everywhere in Washington.
- **Holding deposit.** Capped at 25% of the first month's rent and credited to the first month's rent or the deposit (W12); the ledger shows the credit.
- **Fee in lieu of a deposit.** Out of scope; the app flags the tenancy and does not run a close-out for it (W13).
- **Property sold or foreclosed.** Transfer to the successor's trust account with notice of the new depository, or refund (W9).
- **Domestic violence, sexual assault, stalking or unlawful harassment termination (59.18.575).** The tenant gets the full deposit back "notwithstanding lease provisions that allow for forfeiture of a deposit for early termination", subject to 59.18.280, so documented damage and rent-owing deductions still run through the normal statement but no early-termination forfeiture may be taken; the tenant stays liable for rent for the month of the quitting date unless the termination followed 59.18.200(1) notice (59.18.575(2)(a)-(b)). If the landlord seeks reimbursement from the landlord mitigation program, no part of the deposit may be kept and no suit for the excess (W26). Other tenants on the lease, other than victim household members, stay bound (59.18.575(2)(c)).
- **Tenant dies (sole occupant, 59.18.595).** The statement and refund go to the tenant representative within the 59.18.280 period after the representative removes the property. The app needs the representative's name, address, the proof they gave (personal representative, successor affidavit, designated person) and the removal date (W17, section C).
- **Tenant terminates for cause under the chapter.** A tenant who quits for the landlord's failure to repair (59.18.090), threats by a neighbor under a protective order (59.18.352) or the landlord's threats (59.18.354) is owed a pro rata refund of prepaid rent and the full 59.18.280 statement and refund; no early-termination forfeiture. The app runs the normal close-out.
- **Condemnation or no-occupancy order (59.18.085).** The entire deposit and prepaid rent go back, within seven days by certified check when relocation assistance is owed. Out of scope; flag and stop.
- **Portable cooling device damage (59.18.740(11), from 2026-06-11).** An ordinary damage deduction under 59.18.280 with the usual checklist line and documents; the landlord may not have charged a fee for the device itself.
- **Day 30 falls on a weekend or holiday.** RCW 1.12.040 arguably extends to the next business day; the app does not rely on it (section C).
- **Month-to-month tenant asked for a bigger deposit than fixed-term tenants.** Parity flag (W14).
- **Roommates.** Statute silent; one statement per adult at each address given, one refund to the paying account unless every adult signs an allocation (section G).
- **Deductions exceed the deposit.** The statement shows a claim; the three-year limit applies to leases starting on or after 2023-07-23; undocumented or wear items may not be charged, reported or sent to collections (W26, W27).
- **Email only.** Not compliance. The app refuses to mark delivery done on an email alone (W18).
- **Local cap exceeded (Seattle, Tacoma, Bellingham, Olympia).** Flag at intake with the city rule and the amount over; no local module yet (section L).

## K. Disclaimers and UPL guardrails

**Product copy:**
- "This tool organizes your records and fills in templates based on Washington's Residential Landlord-Tenant Act (chapter 59.18 RCW) and published guidance. It does not give legal advice, does not evaluate your specific situation, and is not a substitute for a Washington attorney." (RCW 2.48.180)
- "Items marked Common practice (useful-life proration, paint schedules) are not in the Washington statute. A court may decide differently."
- "You decide every deduction and approve every document before it is sent. The tool flags rules; it does not decide disputes."
- "Seattle, Tacoma, Bellingham, Olympia and other cities add caps, installment rules and fee limits. Check your city's rental housing program."

**Design rules:**
- Present rules and the statutory text, never "you should deduct".
- Flags, not conclusions: "This item may be wear resulting from ordinary use under RCW 59.18.030(39)".
- Do not answer "can I win in court" questions; route them to WashingtonLawHelp and the county bar's lawyer referral.
- Keep templates generic and let the landlord select and complete them. GR 24(a) defines the practice of law to include "giving advice or counsel to others as to their legal rights" and the "selection, drafting, or completion of legal documents or agreements which affect the legal rights" of another; GR 24(b)(8) permits the "sale of legal forms in any format" and GR 24(d) preserves "information of a general nature about the law and legal procedures" [S22]. Completing a statutory form at the landlord's direction with the statute quoted is the line the product stays behind.
- No Washington court has decided whether software that explains statutory rules gives "advice". Keep every explanation tied to a quoted source.
- Say 30 days and cite 2023 c 331. Many landlord guides and some legal-aid pages still say 21 days (the 2016 to 2023 wording), so the app's copy carries the citation, and the agent never takes a deadline from memory or from a secondary page.

## M. Open questions

1. When a tenant vacates before a fixed term ends and the landlord keeps the lease open, does the 30-day clock run from the move-out or the lease end? The statute says "termination ... and vacation"; no appellate decision was found. The app counts from the move-out and warns.
2. Electronic refunds: nothing in chapter 59.18 allows or forbids them, and no Washington authority on a PayPal or bank refund as "payment ... delivered" was found. The app requires the tenant's written agreement and keeps the paper statement in the safe harbor. RCW 1.80.070 (the Uniform Electronic Transactions Act) adds a wrinkle: a record that another law requires to be sent "by a specified method" must be sent that way, except that a requirement to use "regular United States mail may be varied by agreement to the extent permitted by the other law" (1.80.070(2)(b)(ii), (4)(b)); because 59.18.280(1)(a) is a safe harbor rather than a mandate, whether it is a "requirement" that can be varied is itself unsettled [S43].
3. Electronic signatures on the move-in checklist: chapter 1.80 RCW (Uniform Electronic Transactions Act, 2020 c 57) should make them valid, but no case applies it to 59.18.260(3). The app records who signed, how and when.
4. Joint tenants: the statute names "the tenant" only. Who gets the statement and the refund with roommates is practice, not statute.
5. Resolved in verification: 59.18.670 is 2022 c 81 s 1 (ESHB 2064), effective June 9, 2022. The rule stays out of scope for the app.
6. Seattle: resolved for 7.24.030, .035, .036, .038 and .060, whose text was read on 2026-10-03 (twice). CB 121254's legislation text (Version 3 on Legistar) was read in the fourth pass; only its passage and signing dates still rest on the Mayor's office and press reports, because the Legistar text carries blank signature lines. Whether Version 3 is the version as signed was not confirmed.
7. Resolved in the fourth pass: the current TMC 1.100.040 on eCode360, as amended by Subst. Ord. 29086 (2025-12-09), keeps the one-month move-in cap and the 25% pet damage deposit and drops the late-fee item (now 1.95.065.A, 1.5%). Litigation over the Initiative still not checked.
8. Resolved in the third pass: Olympia is OMC 5.82.050 and 5.82.060, read from the city's code host (Revised 5/26).
9. Whether photos attached to the checklist satisfy "reasonably documented in the written checklist" when the written line is thin. The app requires the written line and treats photos as support.
10. Useful-life proration has no basis in the Washington statute; the only pricing limit is the damaged portion (59.18.280(1)(c)(iv)). Decide whether the engine shows proration for Washington at all, and if so as "Common practice".
11. Whether 59.18.700(4)(a) ("terms of payment or other material conditions") reaches deposit amounts and terms; the app treats it as a warning.
12. Recheck the official text before the Nov 12 submission for any 2026 special-session change; as of 2026-10-03 the 2026 session laws touching chapter 59.18 (c 55, c 184, c 234) add no deposit rule (c 184 only cross-references 59.18.280).
13. Computation of time: whether RCW 1.12.040 extends a day 30 that falls on a weekend or holiday to the next business day for 59.18.280. The Reviser's note says the courts treat 1.12.040 as of general application, but no decision applies it to this section. The app counts calendar days and flags the weekend case.
14. Deceased tenant: 59.18.595 runs the deposit clock from the tenant representative's removal of the property. What happens when no representative ever appears and the landlord disposes of the property under 59.18.595(3) is not stated; the app treats the disposal date as the trigger and warns.
15. Resolved in the third pass: 2026 c 184 (ESSB 6200) is effective June 11, 2026 and 2026 c 234 (SSB 6237) is effective June 11, 2026, both from the Legislature's bill summary pages.
16. Bellingham: the ordinance text (AB 24501) was read; the codified BMC 6.16 page was not (bot wall), so the codifier's lettering of the items in 6.16.030 and 6.16.040 is unverified. Olympia's code host blocked the fourth pass the same way; its text stands as the third pass read it.
