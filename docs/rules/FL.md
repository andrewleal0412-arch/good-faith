This is the rulebook the app enforces for Florida. The product never gives legal advice; it enforces, warns, informs and drafts for the landlord to approve (section K).

**Status of the law.** Security deposits are governed by the Florida Residential Landlord and Tenant Act, Part II of Chapter 83 (ss. 83.40 to 83.683), and within it by s. 83.49, "Deposit money or advance rent; duty of landlord and tenant" [S1]. The section was last amended by s. 2, ch. 2025-16 (CS/CS/CS/HB 615), approved April 29, 2025, effective July 1, 2025 [S5]. That act:
- created s. 83.505, which lets either party receive the notices Part II requires by e-mail once both have signed a specific addendum;
- rewrote 83.49(3)(a) so the notice of intention to impose a claim may go "by certified mail to the tenant's last known mailing address or by e-mail in accordance with s. 83.505", and restated the two deadlines as "within 15 days after the termination of the rental agreement" (refund with no claim) and "within 30 days after the termination of the rental agreement" (the claim notice); the old text said the landlord "shall have 15 days" and "shall have 30 days" measured from vacating;
- let the 83.49(2) holding notice go "in person or delivered by mail or e-mail in accordance with s. 83.505", updated the capitalized disclosure in (2)(d) to match, and changed "lease" to "rental agreement" in (2)(d), (3)(a) and (5) and "shall" to "must" in the subsections it amended (the opening words of (2), "in the lease agreement", were left alone), without changing the forfeiture rule, the tenant's 15-day objection, the 30-day remittance or the substance of subsection (5). The act's title describes its changes to 83.49 as "conforming provisions to changes made by the act; making technical changes", and the House and Senate staff analyses discuss only the e-mail option, never the restated deadlines [S5][S22].

Before that, s. 2, ch. 2024-199 (CS/HB 1305), effective May 17, 2024 "upon becoming a law", replaced "Florida banking institution" with "Florida financial institution" in 83.49(1)(a) and (b) and added the definition at 83.43(7): a bank, credit union, trust company, savings bank or thrift chartered by the United States, Florida or another state, authorized to transact business in Florida, with FDIC or NCUSIF insured deposits [S4]. The 2019 and 2023 texts of 83.49 are identical; the last change before 2024 was s. 3, ch. 2013-136. The 2026 Florida Statutes carry the 2025 text unchanged: no law of the 2026 regular session amended 83.49, and no subsection carries a future effective date [S1]. Two 2026 regular-session laws touched Part II without reaching 83.49: ch. 2026-143 (CS/HB 1293, approved 2026-06-12, effective 2026-10-01) added fraudulent entry of a dwelling under new s. 817.537 to the non-curable noncompliances in 83.56(2)(a), and ch. 2026-125 created s. 83.684, a four-county Homes for Veterans property management pilot [S20]. The three 2026 special sessions (D, E and F: congressional redistricting and consumer protection, a tax package, and the homestead exemption) passed nothing on residential tenancies, per press and Senate summaries (secondary) [S20]. The Senate's citation index lists no 2026 or 2027 bill citing 83.49 as of 2026-10-03, and three 2025 bills: SB 1164 (the Senate companion of HB 615, laid on the table), SB 1562 (a one-month cap on pre-occupancy deposits, died in Judiciary 2025-06-16) and SB 1592 (the "Keep Floridians Housed Act", died in Community Affairs 2025-06-16) [S21].

Around it, since 2019:
- s. 83.43 (definitions) was reordered alphabetically by the reviser's bill, ch. 2023-8 (SB 32), with no change of substance, and gained the "Florida financial institution" definition in 2024 [S2][S4]. "Security deposits" is now 83.43(14), "Deposit money" 83.43(4), "Advance rent" 83.43(2).
- s. 83.491 (fee in lieu of security deposit) was created by ch. 2023-181 (HB 133) for rental agreements entered into or renewed on or after July 1, 2023, and its cross-reference was touched by ch. 2024-199 [S6].
- s. 83.425 (preemption) was created by ch. 2023-314 (CS/HB 1417), effective July 1, 2023: "The regulation of residential tenancies, the landlord-tenant relationship, and all other matters covered under this part are preempted to the state", naming "security deposits" among the matters it supersedes [S7][S8]. The same act moved the month-to-month termination notice in 83.57 from 15 to 30 days.
- s. 83.50 (landlord's address), 83.51, 83.56(4) and 83.575 were conformed to 83.505 by ch. 2025-16 [S5].

Florida has **no deposit cap, no move-in checklist statute, no pre-move-out inspection, no itemization statute and no documentation rule.** What it has instead is a holding regime (a separate account at a Florida financial institution, or a surety bond, with interest owed in two of the three options), a disclosure of where the money is held for landlords of five or more units, a two-track close-out (refund within 15 days when there is no claim, or a certified-mail or 83.505 e-mail notice of the claim within 30 days, in statutory words, followed by the tenant's 15-day objection window and the balance within 30 days of the notice), and one penalty with teeth: a late or missing notice forfeits the claim and any setoff against the deposit.

**Why this matters for PayPal.** Three things change from the California and Texas flows. First, a deposit paid through a PayPal invoice lands in the landlord's PayPal balance, which is not "a separate ... account in a Florida financial institution" and sits next to the landlord's other receipts; 83.49(1) forbids commingling, so the app must have the landlord move the money into the separate account (or confirm a surety bond) and record the depository for the 83.49(2) notice (F4, F6). Second, the statute says nothing about how the refund itself travels, so a PayPal refund of the deposit invoice or a Payout is lawful with no written agreement; the constraints are the dates (F8, F11) and that the deposit, with any interest owed, reaches the tenant in full when there is no claim. Third, the itemized statement the app already produces is not the statutory notice: a Florida claim needs the 83.49(3)(a) wording, sent by certified mail to the last known mailing address or by 83.505 e-mail under a signed addendum, and the landlord may not deduct until 15 days after the tenant receives it (F9, F11). Missing the 30th day costs the whole claim (F10).

## B. What the app must collect at move-in

1. The lease PDF, the date it was signed or last renewed, and the landlord's 83.50 disclosure (name and address for notices). The renewal date decides whether 83.491 and 83.575(1) apply and resets the deposit under 83.49(6) (F15, F22, F25, F28).
2. Every charge at the start with its label and purpose: security deposit, pet or other deposit, advance rent (and which periods), fees. The engine classifies them under 83.43 (F2) and warns on a "nonrefundable" label.
3. How the deposit is held: option (a), (b) or (c) of 83.49(1); the depository's name and address, or the bond's clerk and county; the interest election and the account's rate under (b). Then the confirmation that the PayPal payment was moved into that account, with the date (F4). The app sets a short transfer task (product policy; the statute names no grace period, open question 7).
4. The number of individual dwelling units the landlord rents in Florida, which decides whether the 83.49(2) notice is required (F6). The notice is drafted either way; for five or more units it is a deadline.
5. The 83.505 addendum, if the parties want e-mail: both elections (the landlord's and the tenant's are separate), each party's e-mail address, and the date signed. Without the tenant's election, the F9 notice must go by certified mail (section D).
6. The tenant's mailing address at signing and the instruction, on the tenant page, that a forwarding address must be given before moving out (F13, F14). The 7-day notice under 83.49(5) is the tenant's duty; the tenant page's moving-out form produces a dated written notice with the address.
7. Any 83.595(4) liquidated-damages or early-termination addendum, with its amount (F21), and any 83.575 end-of-term notice clause (F22).
8. Servicemember status, so a qualifying termination blocks the early-termination charges (F23).
9. Move-in photos, room by room, and a condition record. Florida requires neither; the FDACS page recommends dated photos at move-in [S9]. The app collects them because a landlord claiming damages carries the ordinary burden of proof ("As in any other lawsuit, the landlord must sustain his burden of proof in order to prevail", Durene, said of the landlord's separate damages action) and because the fee-shifting in 83.49(3)(c) makes a lost dispute expensive.
10. The refund method. The statute is silent. The app records that the deposit was paid through the PayPal invoice and tells the tenants the refund returns the same way; a Payout to another account needs the tenant's written designation (section G).
11. Whether the tenancy is subsidized or otherwise rent-regulated (F1), and whether the landlord or manager is a ch. 509 licensee or a ch. 475 broker (F17, F18).
12. The names of every adult tenant and, as practice, who receives the refund when there are several (open question 11).

## C. The deadline and what starts the clock

- **Two tracks.** No claim: the deposit, with any interest owed, is returned "within 15 days after the termination of the rental agreement". A claim: the written notice goes out "within 30 days after the termination of the rental agreement" (83.49(3)(a)). Then the tenant has 15 days after receipt to object, and the landlord remits the balance "within 30 days after the date of the notice" (83.49(3)(b)). A clean close-out with a claim can therefore run about 60 days from termination.
- **The trigger.** The sentence opens "Upon the vacating of the premises for termination of the rental agreement", and the 2025 wording counts from "the termination of the rental agreement". For a tenant who leaves on the lease's last day the two dates coincide. When they differ (an early move-out, a holdover, an abandonment), the statute does not say which controls. The pre-2025 text counted from vacating, Form 12 and the (2)(d) disclosure say "after you move out", the act that restated the deadlines calls its 83.49 changes "conforming" and "technical" in its title and its staff analyses never mention the restatement [S5][S22], and no decision on the 2025 wording was found. The app counts from the **earlier** of the move-out date (possession returned) and the termination date, which is the conservative reading, and records both (open question 1).
- **Counting.** Calendar days. Part II has no rule for computing a period; 83.43(10) defines "legal holiday" (holidays observed by the clerk of the court), a term Part II uses in the 83.56(3) three-day notice and not in 83.49. Fla. R. Gen. Prac. & Jud. Admin. 2.514(a) applies to "any ... statute that does not specify a method of computing time" and runs a period ending on a Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday to the next business day, but it is a court rule and whether a court would apply it to a landlord's 83.49 deadline was not confirmed [S14]. The app shows the calendar day and treats the weekend extension as unavailable (open question 2).
- **Receipt.** The tenant's 15 days run from receipt of the notice. For certified mail the app records the USPS delivery date from the tracking number; an unclaimed or refused letter is an open question (4). For 83.505 e-mail, the notice "is deemed delivered at the time it is sent, unless the e-mail is returned to the sender as undeliverable" (83.505(4)), so the 15 days run from sending and the app keeps the copy and the transmission evidence 83.505(5) requires.
- **No tenant notice.** When the tenant did not give the 7-day notice with an address under 83.49(5), the landlord is relieved of the (3)(a) notice requirement, but not of returning the deposit. The app records that fact and still sends the notice to the last known address (F13).
- **Lateness.** Day 31 without a notice on record ends every deduction (F10). Late is not better than never here; the forfeiture is immediate and total for the deposit, and the landlord's only route is a separate damages action after returning the deposit.

## D. How the statement must be delivered

- **The statutory notice** (the claim) travels "by certified mail to the tenant's last known mailing address or by e-mail in accordance with s. 83.505" (83.49(3)(a)). Personal delivery is not named for this notice. The capitalized disclosure in 83.49(2)(d) tells tenants the landlord "MUST PROVIDE YOU WRITTEN NOTICE IN PERSON, BY MAIL, OR BY E-MAIL IN ACCORDANCE WITH SECTION 83.505", which is broader than (3)(a); the app follows (3)(a) and treats hand delivery as a courtesy copy only (open question 5).
- **E-mail** needs the 83.505 addendum: both parties' elections in the statutory form, a valid e-mail address for each, the conspicuous statement that the election is voluntary and revocable; either party may revoke or change the address by written notice, effective on delivery, without invalidating notices already sent; the sender keeps a copy and evidence of transmission; the section "does not preclude service of notices by any other means permitted by law" (83.505(1)-(6)). The app unlocks e-mail as the delivery of record only when the tenant's election is on file and not revoked.
- **Certified mail** is the default. The app's delivery record stores the tracking number, the mailing date (which must be on or before day 30) and the delivery date (which starts the 15 days). Return receipt is practice, not statute.
- **Address rule.** The last known mailing address: the forwarding address from the tenant's 7-day notice or the tenant page, else the vacated unit (F14). Unlike Texas, Florida never suspends the 15-day return duty for a missing address; the only relief is from the (3)(a) notice requirement, and only when the tenant skipped the 83.49(5) notice (F13). The clock runs regardless.
- **The holding notice** (F6) may go "in person or delivered by mail or e-mail in accordance with s. 83.505" (83.49(2)(a)), or sit in the lease itself. It must "Contain the following disclosure" (83.49(2)(d)); the app prints it word for word in the draft (`FL_HOLDING_DISCLOSURE` in `src/lib/states/fl.ts`). As the 2026 Florida Statutes print it (flsenate.gov, read 2026-10-03, as amended by ch. 2025-16):

  > YOUR RENTAL AGREEMENT REQUIRES PAYMENT OF CERTAIN DEPOSITS. THE LANDLORD MAY TRANSFER ADVANCE RENTS TO THE LANDLORD'S ACCOUNT AS THEY ARE DUE AND WITHOUT NOTICE. WHEN YOU MOVE OUT, YOU MUST GIVE THE LANDLORD YOUR NEW ADDRESS SO THAT THE LANDLORD CAN SEND YOU NOTICES REGARDING YOUR DEPOSIT. THE LANDLORD MUST PROVIDE YOU WRITTEN NOTICE IN PERSON, BY MAIL, OR BY E-MAIL IN ACCORDANCE WITH SECTION 83.505, FLORIDA STATUTES, WITHIN 30 DAYS AFTER YOU MOVE OUT, OF THE LANDLORD'S INTENT TO IMPOSE A CLAIM AGAINST THE DEPOSIT. IF YOU DO NOT REPLY TO THE LANDLORD STATING YOUR OBJECTION TO THE CLAIM WITHIN 15 DAYS AFTER RECEIPT OF THE LANDLORD'S WRITTEN NOTICE, THE LANDLORD WILL COLLECT THE CLAIM AND MUST MAIL YOU THE REMAINING DEPOSIT, IF ANY.
  >
  > IF THE LANDLORD FAILS TO TIMELY PROVIDE YOU NOTICE, THE LANDLORD MUST RETURN THE DEPOSIT BUT MAY LATER FILE A LAWSUIT AGAINST YOU FOR DAMAGES. IF YOU FAIL TO TIMELY OBJECT TO A CLAIM, THE LANDLORD MAY COLLECT FROM THE DEPOSIT, BUT YOU MAY LATER FILE A LAWSUIT CLAIMING A REFUND.
  >
  > YOU SHOULD ATTEMPT TO INFORMALLY RESOLVE ANY DISPUTE BEFORE FILING A LAWSUIT. GENERALLY, THE PARTY IN WHOSE FAVOR A JUDGMENT IS RENDERED WILL BE AWARDED COSTS AND ATTORNEY FEES PAYABLE BY THE LOSING PARTY.
  >
  > THIS DISCLOSURE IS BASIC. PLEASE REFER TO PART II OF CHAPTER 83, FLORIDA STATUTES, TO DETERMINE YOUR LEGAL RIGHTS AND OBLIGATIONS.
- **The itemized statement and packet** the app produces are attached to the claim notice as its "reason" and documentation. They do not replace the statutory statement, which the notice must contain "in substantially the following form"; a county court has rejected a claim whose notice lacked the form language (Duvall v. Sunshine State Realty & Associates, Inc., No. 2012-21898, Volusia County Court, Feb. 27, 2013, an $800 claim, reported second-hand on a practitioner's blog and not verified [S15]).

## E. Documentation rules

- Statute: the claim notice states the amount and "the reason for imposing the claim" (83.49(3)(a)). No itemization, receipts, invoices, photos or dollar threshold are required for a deposit claim. California's $125 rule and photo duties have no Florida counterpart.
- The only documentation list in Part II is for a fee-in-lieu insurer claim: "an itemized list of any unpaid amounts and the dates such amounts were due, documentation supporting any itemized damages and costs of repairs, and a copy of any written objection" (83.491(2)(b)1). The app uses that shape for every deposit claim as a practice: one line per item, the due dates for rent, the invoice or labor description, and the tenant's objection if any.
- Burden and fees: a landlord claiming damages carries the ordinary burden of proof ("As in any other lawsuit, the landlord must sustain his burden of proof in order to prevail", Durene, on the separate damages action), and the loser pays costs and a reasonable attorney fee (83.49(3)(c); 83.48). The app keeps the California packet structure as evidence, labeled "Evidence, not required by Florida law": before and after photos for repair and cleaning items, the vendor invoice or the landlord's hours and rate, the item's age for any proration.
- Estimates: the statute is silent. The notice may state a claim for an amount not yet invoiced; the app marks estimates and follows up with the invoice, as a practice, and never raises the amount after the notice without a new notice inside the 30 days (open question 13).

## F. Penalties

- **Forfeiture.** A landlord who does not give the written notice within 30 days "forfeits the right to impose a claim upon the security deposit and may not seek a setoff against the deposit" and may only "file an action for damages after returning the security deposit to the tenant" (83.49(3)(a)). Durene v. Alcime describes three penalties: the loss of "that particular, readily available fund of money", liability for fees and costs under (3)(c), and the burden and collection risk of a separate suit; "The security deposit may not be used for purposes of a set-off" [S13].
- **Fees and costs.** The prevailing party in an action over the deposit recovers court costs and a reasonable attorney fee, and the court advances the case (83.49(3)(c)); the general rule for any action under the lease or Part II is the same and cannot be waived (83.48).
- **Damages.** Actual damages for noncompliance (83.55); any Part II duty is enforceable by civil action (83.54). Florida has **no statutory multiple** for a wrongfully kept deposit (contrast California's up to twice the deposit and Texas's $100 plus three times).
- **Holding violations.** Commingling or using the deposit breaks 83.49(1); the statute states no specific penalty beyond the general remedies (a surety bond under (1)(c) "shall run to the Governor for the benefit of any tenant injured by the landlord's violation"), plus license discipline for ch. 509 licensees (83.49(8)) and, for brokers, the ch. 475 regime that 83.49(3)(d) says this section satisfies.
- **Tenant side.** A tenant who skips the 7-day notice loses the landlord's notice duty, not the deposit (83.49(5)); a tenant who does not object in time keeps the right to sue (83.49(3)(b)).
- **Forum.** County court; small claims rules apply to demands up to $8,000 exclusive of costs, interest and attorney fees (Fla. Sm. Cl. R. 7.010(b); the $8,000 figure dates from the amendment effective 2020-01-01 and is repeated in the Supreme Court's latest order on the rule, No. SC2024-1765 (Fla. Oct. 23, 2025), effective 2026-01-01 [S16]).

## G. How the refund may be paid

- **Statute.** None on method. The deposit must be returned "together with interest if otherwise required" within 15 days when there is no claim, and the balance remitted within 30 days after the notice when there is one. Any method that gets the money to the tenant by then satisfies the text.
- **PayPal refund of the deposit invoice.** Allowed; no tenant agreement is required by Florida law. Two Florida-specific points: the deposit should have left the PayPal balance for the separate account during the tenancy (F4), so the refund is funded by moving the money back or from the linked bank account; and there is no mailing presumption in Florida, so the app prints PayPal's refund id, amount and timestamp on the statement as the proof of date.
- **Payout to a different account or e-mail.** Allowed by statute; a payment to the wrong payee is a failure to return the deposit in substance. The app requires the tenant's written designation first (product policy, not law).
- **Interest.** Under options (b) and (c) the accrued interest since the last annual payment is added to the refund and shown as its own line (F7). Under option (a) there is none.
- **Several tenants on one lease.** The statute holds the money "for the benefit of the tenant or tenants" and says nothing about the payee. The lease controls; the app refunds the paying PayPal account by default and splits only under a written allocation signed by every adult tenant, as practice (open question 11).
- **Timing with a claim.** Nothing may be deducted before the tenant's 15 days after receipt have run; the balance leaves by the 30th day after the notice date. The app releases the undisputed balance early when the landlord chooses to, which the statute permits.
- **Deposit paid by a third party.** Flag it; refund per the lease to the tenant unless the payer is a party to the lease.

## H. Wear and tear

Part II never defines "normal wear and tear" and the deposit section never uses the phrase. The concept enters by implication: the deposit secures "the performance of the rental agreement" and "monetary damage ... caused by the tenant's breach" (83.43(14)); the tenant's duties are to keep the premises "clean and sanitary" and to "Not destroy, deface, damage, impair, or remove any part of the premises" (83.52(2), (6)); ordinary deterioration from living in the unit breaks neither, so there is no damage to charge. The fee-in-lieu section uses the phrase five times, each time limiting the tenant's exposure to "damage to the premises beyond normal wear and tear" (83.491(2)(a), (2)(b), (3)(h), (3)(i) and the capitalized disclosure in (4)(b)).

Florida publishes no official wear-and-tear guide comparable to California's DRE guide. The FDACS page tells tenants to photograph the unit at move-in and note damaged items [S9]; the Florida Bar pamphlet says nothing on the point [S10]. Useful-life proration and paint schedules are not in Florida law; the app may keep its California proration as "Common practice", labeled so, because a prorated charge is easier to defend in a suit where the landlord bears the burden and the loser pays fees. Confidence: H = statute, L = common practice.

| Item | Usually | Source, confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Faded or scuffed paint, small nail holes, worn carpet paths, loose blinds from age, dusty fixtures | Not deductible: no breach of 83.52, so no "monetary damage" | 83.52, 83.43(14) by implication, H on the principle; examples L |
| Large holes, gouges, burns, pet urine, broken fixtures, chewed trim | Deductible: damage to the premises | 83.52(6), H |
| Dirty oven, mildew, trash left behind, unwashed floors | Deductible cleaning, to the "clean and sanitary" standard | 83.52(2)-(3), H; the cost must be reasonable, L |
| A flat cleaning fee regardless of condition | Not a damage item; a fee that secures performance is deposit money (F2) and a charge with no breach behind it is a W | 83.43(4), (14), H; reading L |
| Damage from a prior tenant, or cumulative wear across tenancies | Not deductible: not this tenant's breach | 83.43(14), H |
| Replacement of an aged item the tenant damaged | Deductible; proration by remaining life is common practice, not statute | 83.52(6), H; proration L |

## I. Notice and statement checklist

The claim notice (F9) and the itemized statement that travels with it:

1. Tenant name(s) and the last known mailing address used; the unit; the move-out date and the termination date; the day the tenant's 7-day notice arrived, or "none received". 83.49(3)(a), (5)
2. The statutory statement, verbatim in substance, with the total claimed in the first blank, the reason in the second, and the landlord's 83.50 notice address in the last. 83.49(3)(a); 83.50
3. The notice date and the method: certified mail with the tracking number, or 83.505 e-mail with the addendum date and the sending time. 83.49(3)(a); 83.505(4)-(5)
4. The two dates the tenant needs: 15 days after receipt to object in writing, and the day the balance is due (30 days after the notice date). 83.49(3)(b)
5. Deposit received: amount, date, how paid (PayPal invoice id); advance rent and other deposits listed separately; how it was held and any interest owed. 83.43; 83.49(1), (9)
6. One line per item: description, amount, basis (unpaid rent with due dates; damage; cleaning; lease clause quoted or breach), marked estimate where applicable. 83.43(14); 83.52 (practice shape from 83.491(2)(b)1)
7. Wear-and-tear findings listed at $0, so the tenant sees they were considered and excluded. Section H
8. Evidence attached and labeled as evidence: photos, invoices, labor description, item age. Durene (burden); 83.49(3)(c) (fees)
9. Balance to be refunded, method (PayPal refund id and timestamp, or check), payee(s), and the interest line. 83.49(3)(b), (9)
10. If deductions exceed the deposit: the balance claimed, stated as a claim, not a demand. 83.43(14)
11. The 83.49(3)(c) sentence in plain words: either party may sue over the deposit, the prevailing party recovers costs and fees, and the court advances the case. The (2)(d) line: "YOU SHOULD ATTEMPT TO INFORMALLY RESOLVE ANY DISPUTE BEFORE FILING A LAWSUIT."
12. The disclaimer line: templates and records, not legal advice. Section K

For a no-claim close-out the statement is simpler: deposit, interest, refund method and date, within 15 days (F8); the statute requires no document at all in that case, so the statement is a receipt.

## J. Edge cases

- **The money is still in PayPal at move-out.** The holding rule was broken during the tenancy; nothing in 83.49 forgives or cures it, and nothing changes the close-out dates. The app flags it in the audit log and completes the refund on time; the exposure is actual damages under 83.55, not a multiple.
- **Tenant left without the 7-day notice or an address.** The landlord's (3)(a) notice duty is relieved (F13). The app still sends the notice to the unit address within 30 days and records why: the deposit must still be returned, and a landlord who sends the notice anyway loses nothing.
- **Notice mailed on day 30, received on day 35.** Timely: the 30 days govern giving the notice, and the tenant's 15 days run from receipt. The balance is still due 30 days after the notice date, so the window to deduct is short; the app shows both dates.
- **Tenant objects on day 10.** No deduction by the agent. The undisputed balance goes out on schedule; the disputed amount stays in the separate account; the dispute card shows 83.49(3)(c) and county court self-help. No advice on the merits.
- **Tenant objects on day 20.** Late under (3)(b): the landlord "may then deduct" after day 15. The tenant keeps the right to sue; the app records the late objection with the deduction and tells the landlord so.
- **Notice missed.** Day 31: deductions blocked, full refund plus interest, damages memo offered (F10). The landlord's separate suit is outside this tool.
- **Early move-out.** 83.595 governs the landlord's choice; a liquidated-damages or early-termination deduction needs the signed addendum and the 2-month cap (F21); otherwise rent through the month of retaking plus damage, or the reletting shortfall, each stated as a claim on the notice. Interest is not owed to a tenant who "wrongfully terminates" (83.49(9)). The clock question in section C applies.
- **Servicemember termination.** Prorated rent and damage only (F23).
- **Renewal.** A new agreement and a new deposit (83.49(6)): re-issue the holding disclosure for five or more units, restart the interest year, keep the same account.
- **Owner sold during the tenancy.** 83.49(7) workflow: transfer with interest and an accounting, written receipt, successor's one-month presumption.
- **Fee in lieu of a deposit.** Out of scope; the lease check flags it (F25).
- **Section 8 or public housing.** 83.49(4) may take the tenancy out of the section (F1); the app flags and defers to the program's rules.
- **Several tenants.** Payee per the lease; split only under a signed allocation (section G).
- **Lease signed before July 1, 2025 with no 83.505 addendum.** E-mail is not the delivery of record; certified mail is. 83.505 attaches to the rental agreement rather than to its signing date, so the addendum may be added to an existing lease; the app asks for it before the tenancy ends, because an "addendum to the rental agreement" signed after the agreement has terminated is untested (open question 17).
- **Deductions exceed the deposit.** The notice states the full claim; the tool drafts only. No collections, no credit reporting.
- **Late fees.** No Part II statute caps or regulates a residential late fee (California's liquidated-damages rule, D26, has no Florida statutory counterpart). A late fee may be deducted only as a lease charge with the clause quoted (F19); 83.43(12) lets a written rental agreement designate other payments as "rent". Whether a given fee is an enforceable liquidated-damages clause or an unenforceable penalty is common law, not researched: W, with the clause and the amount shown on the notice.

## K. Disclaimers and UPL guardrails

**Product copy:**
- "This tool organizes your records and fills in templates based on Florida Statutes Chapter 83, Part II, including section 83.49. It does not give legal advice, does not evaluate your specific situation, and is not a substitute for the advice of a Florida attorney."
- "Items marked Common practice (useful-life proration, paint schedules, joint refunds, the evidence packet) are not in the statute. A court may decide differently."
- "You decide every deduction and approve every document before it is sent. The tool flags rules; it does not decide disputes."
- "Not legal advice."

**Design rules:**
- Present rules and the statutory text, never "you should deduct".
- Flags, not conclusions ("This item may be ordinary wear and tear; Florida law lets the deposit cover damage and unpaid obligations, not normal use").
- Do not answer "can I win in court" questions; route to the county court's self-help pages and lawyer referral.
- Keep templates generic and let the landlord select and complete them. The Florida Bar's own Form 12 is a fill-in form approved under rule 10-2.1(a) with the warning that forms "are not a substitute for the advice of an attorney" [S11][S12]; the app's notice tracks the current statute rather than the 2010 form and carries the same warning. The Florida Supreme Court's line between selling and completing forms and giving advice (The Florida Bar v. Brumbaugh, 355 So. 2d 1186 (Fla. 1978)) was not re-read for this spec.
- No Florida court has decided whether an AI agent that explains statutory rules is giving advice. Keep every explanation tied to a quoted source.

## L. Local overlays (flag, do not implement)

Since July 1, 2023, s. 83.425 preempts to the state "the regulation of residential tenancies, the landlord-tenant relationship, and all other matters covered under this part", naming security deposits, fees and notice requirements, and the Senate's bill summary says the act "renders all existing local government ordinances throughout the state that purport to regulate residential tenancies ... null and void" [S7][S8]. The engine computes nothing city by city. Local building, housing and health codes, rental registration or inspection programs and business tax receipts sit outside Part II and were not reviewed here. The app flags, by county or city, that a former ordinance existed and no longer applies, so a landlord who remembers it is not misled:

- **Miami-Dade County.** Tenant's Bill of Rights, Ord. 22-47 (May 2022): 60 days' notice of rent increases over 5 percent, a notice of tenant rights, an Office of Housing Advocacy. Preempted 2023-07-01 [S17][S18].
- **City of Miami and Miami Beach.** Miami's 2022 Tenant Bill of Rights and Miami Beach's 2021 ordinance requiring 60 days' written notice of rent increases over 5 percent. Preempted [S17].
- **Broward County.** 2022 Tenant's Bill of Rights, including written notice of late fees before they could be charged and 60 days' notice of rent increases over 5 percent. Preempted; the county now posts ch. 2023-314 on its renters page [S8][S18].
- **Orange County.** Fair Notice Ordinance (2021: 60 days' notice of rent increases over 5 percent) and the 2022 Tenant's Bill of Rights. Preempted; the 2022 rent stabilization ordinance was challenged in court and is not in effect; the county's source-of-income rule was argued to survive as fair housing rather than landlord-tenant regulation (not verified) [S17][S19].
- **Hillsborough County and Tampa.** The county's 2021 Tenant Bill of Rights (source-of-income protection, notice of late fees, notice of rights) and Tampa's March 2022 Tenants Bill of Rights (source of income, a notice of rights and assistance resources). Preempted [S17][S18].
- **St. Petersburg and Pinellas County.** St. Petersburg repealed its 2022 Tenants Bill of Rights by a 6-0 council vote in summer 2023, citing HB 1417; Pinellas County took down its Tenant Bill of Rights page [S19].
- **Everywhere else.** No county or city deposit rule survives 83.425; none is checked or computed.

## M. Open questions

1. **Vacating versus termination.** After ch. 2025-16 the 15 and 30 days run from "the termination of the rental agreement" in a sentence that opens "Upon the vacating of the premises". For an early move-out, holdover or abandonment the two dates differ and no decision on the 2025 wording was found. The app counts from the earlier date and records both.
2. **Weekend or holiday last day.** Whether Fla. R. Gen. Prac. & Jud. Admin. 2.514 extends an 83.49 period that ends on a weekend was not confirmed; the app shows the calendar day.
3. **Objection received.** The statute does not say what the landlord may do after a timely objection, other than that either party may sue and the loser pays fees. The app holds the disputed amount and refunds the rest; that is product policy.
4. **Receipt of certified mail.** When the tenant refuses or never claims the letter, when the 15 days start was not found in a decision. The app records the USPS delivery or the first attempted-delivery date and flags the question.
5. **(2)(d) versus (3)(a).** The disclosure the tenant receives says the claim notice may come "in person, by mail, or by e-mail"; the operative rule says certified mail or 83.505 e-mail. The app follows (3)(a). Whether an uncertified mailing or hand delivery would be upheld as substantial compliance was not researched.
6. **Holding notice on renewal.** 83.49(6) makes a carried-forward deposit "a new security deposit"; whether that re-triggers the 30-day notice in (2) for landlords of five or more units is not stated. The app re-issues it inside the renewal.
7. **PayPal as transit.** Whether a deposit that passes through the landlord's PayPal balance for a few days before reaching the separate account violates 83.49(1)(a) was not found in any decision. The app treats the transfer as due promptly and records the date; the statute names no grace period.
8. **Section 8 and other regulated tenancies.** 83.49(4) removes from the section tenancies where "the amount of rent or deposit, or both, is regulated"; whether a Housing Choice Voucher tenancy falls out of 83.49 entirely was not researched. The app flags it.
9. **Stale cross-reference.** 83.491(6) says a fee in lieu is not a security deposit "as defined in s. 83.43(13)"; after ch. 2024-199 renumbered the definitions, (13) is "Rental agreement" and "Security deposits" is (14). The act created the error itself: s. 3 of ch. 2024-199 changed the reference from (12) to (13) to match the 2023 reorder, while s. 1 of the same act inserted (7) and pushed "Security deposits" to (14) [S4]. The intent is clear; a reviser's bill may fix it. The app cites 83.43(14).
10. **Nonrefundable fees.** The statute neither permits nor bars a "nonrefundable" pet or cleaning fee; 83.43(4) treats a pet deposit as deposit money "held by the landlord on behalf of the tenant". Whether a fee that secures performance is a deposit under 83.43(14) turns on facts; the app warns rather than reclassifies.
11. **Joint tenants.** The statute names no payee among several tenants. The lease controls; the app's joint-refund default is practice.
12. **Decisions.** Durene v. Alcime was decided under the 1981 text, when the notice window was 15 days; no appellate decision on the 30-day text or the 2025 wording was read. The 2013 county court matter rejecting a notice without the form language (Duvall v. Sunshine State Realty & Associates, Inc., No. 2012-21898, Volusia County Court, Feb. 27, 2013) is known only from a practitioner's blog [S15].
13. **Amending the claim.** Whether a landlord may raise or add to the claim after the notice (inside or outside the 30 days) was not researched; the app never changes a sent notice and sends a corrected one only inside the 30 days.
14. **Attorney General page.** myfloridalegal.com returned HTTP 403 twice; the FDACS page and the Florida Bar pamphlet were used instead. The FDACS page describes the claim notice as sent "in person, by mail or by email", tracking (2)(d) rather than (3)(a) [S9].
15. **2027 session.** The statute page links to the Senate's index of 2027 bills citing 83.49; on 2026-10-03 it returned no bills, and the 2026 index returned none either [S21]. Recheck before a Florida launch; prefiling runs into early 2027.
16. **Interest math.** "75 percent of the annualized average interest rate payable on such account" needs the account's actual rate history; the app asks the landlord for it and lets the landlord elect 5 percent simple instead, as the statute allows.
17. **Addendum after termination.** 83.505(1) requires "an addendum to the rental agreement"; whether one signed after the agreement has ended supports e-mail delivery of the claim notice was not found in any source. The app asks for the addendum before move-out.
