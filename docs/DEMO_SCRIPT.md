# Demo video script

Under three minutes, seven beats, one fresh demo copy. Each beat names the screen to show, what to click, and one line to say. The times are a budget, not a stopwatch. `scripts/e2e-demo.py` walks beats 1 to 7 on a production build (with Claude and PayPal stubbed) and checks every figure on the way, so a passing run means the main path below works as written; the optional beats are not in it.

The order follows the money: the move-out first, since that is where a landlord starts and where Claude is seen at work, then the refund it leads to, the tenants' answer, the two agents, and the settlement both sides approve; the lease check and the second state close on what the same engine does before a tenancy starts and outside California. The deposit has no beat of its own: it is the payment the refund review names ("from the 1140 E. Juniper Hollow Ln deposit"), and the close-out agent verifies it with PayPal before it proposes the refund on that same payment, so beat 2 shows it at the moment it matters.

## Before recording

- Open the hosted demo once a few minutes ahead, so Render is awake, and check the pool has a deposit ready (`npm run demo:pool -- --status`).
- Enter the judges' code in a private window: a fresh copy, dated today. Use Start over if you rehearsed in it.
- Hide the demo guide and the next-step bar before the take: the guide's line sits above every page's heading and the bar at the foot of every page, so they would be in every shot. Each one's Hide puts it away for that browser; Demo guide in the account menu brings both back.
- A browser's first visit to Properties opens the welcome card. Skip it (or take its tour) before the take; it opens once per browser, and Welcome tour in the account menu opens it again.
- Two windows side by side: the landlord on the left, the tenants' page on the right. Tenants, in the switch at the top of every demo page, opens the tenants' page in the same tab (recording the statement's delivery first if it hasn't been), so open a second window on the same copy for the landlord, or drag "Open the tenant's view" (a new tab) out to the right.
- Beats 3 and 5 hold seven clicks each in 25 seconds, which fits only without fumbling: the tenants' page asks for their name before Ask my advocate and before Accept settlement, and Send, Accept settlement and Approve and pay each ask once more in a small dialog, naming the amount where there is one. To give beat 3 room, press Tenants in the switch off camera right after beat 2 (it records the statement's delivery and opens the tenants' page), drag that window to the right, and start beat 3 on the tenants' page. Rehearse 3 to 5 once on a spare copy (Start over gives a fresh one).
- The statement and the refund review draw their own rows first and fill PayPal's part in about a second; Record delivery, Prepare refund, Send refund and Decline stay off until it has, so wait for the button to come on rather than clicking into a disabled one.
- Three waits are real Claude or PayPal calls: the close-out agent (under a minute), the advocate (about a minute) and the lease check (about 20 seconds). Record them and cut the wait. Beat 6 also has a take with no wait: Check the sample lease, under the upload box, shows the same flags in one click with no Claude call, from the facts Claude read when the demo was built, with a Sample chip on the result (it is the step the README's Try it leads with). The upload is the take that shows Claude reading live; if you film it and want its result on screen without the wait, run it once before recording.
- If the negotiation runs long or the agents don't agree on camera, beat 4 can use 482 N. Glassell St, Unit B instead, which comes with a settled dispute. Say it is the demo's recorded example. Its beat 5 shows the settlement row and the amended statement, but no PayPal refund id: no PayPal money moved for it, and the page says "Demo example". The PayPal refund then rests on beat 2, so prefer a re-take of the live run for the final cut.
- The motion moments (below) play once per browser for each copy, the first time each thing is seen after a click: rehearse on a spare copy, then record on a fresh one, and don't open the Move-out tab or the refund review of the recording copy before the take. Reduce motion must be off on the recording Mac (System Settings, Accessibility, Display), or nothing moves. A page opened by a full reload shows its moments at rest, so move between screens by clicking, not by reloading.

**The same seven beats inside a demo copy.** A judge's private copy opens with them as a short checklist above each
page: one line each, in this order, each a link to where that beat happens in that copy, and each ticked off by the
copy's own rows (the photo comparison, the refund, the dispute, the negotiation, the settlement, the lease Claude read,
the unit in the second state). The three of those a fresh copy already holds (beats 1, 6 and 7) are ticked only once
that browser has opened their page (beat 6 once a check's result is on it), so the guide opens at none done rather
than three, and sends the judge through the beats where Claude is watched at work. On Properties the checklist shows
all seven; on every other page it is one line, the count and the next beat as a link to its page, until the judge
opens it from its head, so the screen a beat opens is not pushed down by the list that sent them there.
It reads the copy again whenever a click opens another page, so a step that completes in the tenants' tab is ticked
on the landlord's side without a reload.

At the foot of every demo page, the landlord's and the tenants', a bar says the one thing to press next: "Step 2 of 7:
press Prepare refund". Take me there opens the page that control is on, scrolls it into view and rings it with a
steady brand outline until it is pressed; then the bar moves to the next press. While Claude works it says on what
and for about how long ("Claude, the close-out agent, is preparing the refund, usually under a minute"). The bar never
covers what it points at (the page scrolls to leave room for it, at phone width too), and Hide puts it away. At the top
of every demo page, Landlord | Tenants switches sides in one click: Tenants records the statement's delivery first
when it hasn't been (the law's step before the tenants can dispute), then opens their page; Landlord goes back.

The table above is the source of the beats. `src/lib/demo-guide.ts` carries the same seven numbers and titles, names
the screen each one opens, and takes the unit a beat runs on from the address that beat's own row names (beat 1 for the
live unit, beat 7 for the second state's), so nothing a judge does in the copy can move the guide to another unit.
`scripts/e2e-demo.py` reads both files and fails when the numbers, the titles, their order or those two addresses
drift, so a beat renamed, reordered or moved to another unit here has to move there too. The owner's workspace never
shows it.

**Optional beat 1b, the deductions grid and Insights (about 25 seconds, for the AG Grid sponsor prize or a longer cut).** Right after beat 1, on the same Move-out tab, in the Deductions card: click the carpet's Life cell and type 12. The cell refuses it: the tip under it gives the reason with the DRE guide's citation and says Escape keeps 10 years. Press Escape. Click Life again, type 9 and press Enter: the amount flashes to $600.00 and Refund due moves to $3,910.00 under the grid. Then type 10 back the same way, so beat 2 still sends $3,790. Open Insights from the sidebar (the four charts draw in the first time this browser opens it) and tap a state in the state filter: the figures, the four charts and the grid narrow together. Say: "Every money screen is an AG Grid. The landlord edits the inputs in place, the same code the server runs holds each one to the lawful range as it is typed, and the totals move live. Insights reads the same ledger rows as Payments, so the numbers agree." This beat needs the landlord's window at least 1024 wide, where every input is its own cell; in a narrower window the inputs move to Edit in the row's menu.

**Optional beat 1c, the quick read (about 10 seconds; only with `TYPESAFE_API_KEY` on the host).** Right after beat 1, under Bedroom 1 on the same Move-out tab, Add finding, and type "Nail holes in bedroom wall": a moment after the last key the chip under Type reads "Reads as wear and tear, 92%, under Civ. Code 1950.5(b)(2), (e)(2)(A)" and Wear and tear is picked. Clear it and type "Large burn in the carpet": the old pick lets go as soon as the words change, then Damage. Cancel, so the copy keeps its three deductions. Say: "A fast model, Jev, reads my words against California's rules as I type. It picks the type only when it's sure, and my pick always wins." Without the key, Add finding shows the three Type buttons with Damage picked and no chip, and the beat is skipped.

**Optional beat 2b, a refund made in PayPal (about 20 seconds, for a longer cut).** Set up off camera on a second fresh copy (Start over): its move-out unit has its own pre-paid sandbox deposit. Before its close-out, log in to the PayPal sandbox as the business account the app's credentials belong to (developer.paypal.com, Testing Tools, Sandbox Accounts), open the $4,800.00 deposit payment in Activity and refund $1,000.00 of it from there. PayPal's webhook brings it to the property when the host has `PAYPAL_WEBHOOK_ID` set (the sandbox can take a few minutes to deliver); otherwise open the property after a minute, and its next read of PayPal finds it. On camera: the amber "Refund made in PayPal" banner on the Overview, whose Next step reads "Review the refund made in PayPal" and whose header button reads "Review PayPal refund"; that button opens the Payments tab's card, with the amount, the day and PayPal's refund id; press Record as this deposit's refund. Then Review refund: the plan now sends $2,790.00, the $3,790.00 owed less the $1,000.00 already refunded in PayPal, and says so. Say: "A landlord refunded part of it in PayPal by hand. Good Faith hears about it from PayPal, holds the close-out until I say whose refund it was, and then sends only what is still owed, so the same money never goes back twice." The deposit is used afterwards, so `npm run demo:pool -- --fill` once you're done.

**Optional beat 5b, chargeback defense (about 25 seconds, for a longer cut).** Set up off camera on a second fresh copy (Start over): its move-out unit has its own pre-paid sandbox deposit. First send its refund as in beat 2 ($3,790 back; the statement keeps $1,010). Then, as the sandbox tenant, open that refund in the PayPal sandbox's Activity and follow View receipt to the deposit payment (Show invoice details gives its invoice number, which the property's Payments tab shows too). Choose Report a problem, then "I want a refund for some or all of the items in this order", item type Service and category Other, "The seller agreed to issue a refund", $1,010.00, No for contacting the seller, one sentence (the charges are normal wear), and Submit. PayPal reviews it for a few minutes before it opens the dispute to the landlord. File once per payment: a second report on it is merged into the first and can send it back to PayPal's review, and "my account is being used by someone else" makes the sandbox account ask to be secured. The deposit is used afterwards, so `npm run demo:pool -- --fill` once you're done. On camera: the unit's red PayPal dispute chip on Properties, then its Payments tab: Check PayPal, Prepare a response (a Claude call, under a minute; cut the wait). Nothing is owed back by the rules, so evidence is allowed, and the agent sends it: the itemized statement's PDF, each charge's move-in and move-out photos with the box drawn, the invoices, and proof of the $3,790 refund. Then Approve and send. Say: "If the tenants go to PayPal instead, the defense agent answers from the same records: the statement, the boxed photos, the refund. Code won't let it argue for keeping a dollar the law says goes back, and nothing reaches PayPal until I approve it." (On a copy whose refund hasn't gone, the only lawful answer is to give the money back through the dispute: that is the case docs/HOW_IT_WORKS.md, Chargebacks, describes, not the one to film.)

Close back on the Properties page (2:50 to 2:58; the back arrow beside the address): "Good Faith. AI that informs and negotiates inside the law, PayPal that pays only when both sides say yes. Not legal advice."

## The motion on camera

Five deliberate moments (`docs/DESIGN.md`, Motion), each landing in under a second, all calm: no bounce, no confetti. Where each one shows:

| Moment | Beat | What to do on camera | What it shows |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Inspection boxes | 1, Move-out boxes | Click the Move-out tab (from Overview, not a reload) and pause on the first room while its move-out photo loads | Each box's outline is drawn around what changed, one after another in its own color, then its number settles in |
| 2. Negotiation | 4, Two-agent negotiation | Let my negotiator answer, and hold on the thread | Three dots on the side whose agent is writing, only while it really works; each turn opens into the thread as it lands and its check mark settles in after it |
| 3. Quick read | 1c, the quick read | Type "Nail holes in bedroom wall" under Add finding | The chip drops in under Type and its bar fills to the read's likelihood; new words change it in place, without it vanishing |
| 4. Steps | 2, Refund approval | Prepare refund, then scroll down to "Done by the agent" under Details | The agent's steps draw their checks one after another, the line filling down to "Waiting for your approval" |
| 5. Refund sent | 2, Refund approval | Send refund, and hold on the receipt for a second | The green mark scales in and its check draws, $3,790.00 settles, "Sent through PayPal" completes the timeline, the rest fades in |

Smaller motion shows on its own along the way: a page or a tab easing into place, a tab's underline sliding to the tab opened, the status chip changing in place (Approve and pay in beat 5: "Settled"), and in optional beat 1b Refund due landing on $3,910.00 when the carpet's life changes and Insights' charts drawing in. The close-out agent's and the advocate's waits are real Claude calls: cut the wait, but keep the frame where the result lands, since that is where the moment plays.

## What each beat proves

- Beats 1 and 6: Claude reads and classifies; code decides every amount (`rules.ts`, `deductions.ts`).
- Beat 2: agentic commerce with a human approval on every money tool, on the deposit payment the agent verified with PayPal (`agent.ts`, `needsApproval`, `guardRefund`).
- Beats 3 to 5: two agents for two humans, bounded by code (`lawful-range.ts`, `negotiator.ts`), settled through PayPal only after both approve (`settlement.ts`).
- Beat 7: the state packs (`src/lib/states/`, `docs/rules/`).
- Beat 1b: AG Grid and AG Charts Community, the deductions edited in place against the lawful range, and the portfolio in one ledger (`deductions-grid.tsx`, `deduction-edit.ts`, `insights.ts`).
- Beat 1c: the optional quick read of a finding's words (`jev.ts`), off without its key.
- Beat 2b: PayPal's own record kept in step with the app's: a refund made in PayPal noted once, decided by the landlord, and counted once by the close-out (`reconcile.ts`, `outside-refunds.ts`, `closeout.ts`).
- Beat 5b: a PayPal dispute read through the Agent Toolkit, answered by an agent bounded by the lawful range, sent only on approval (`chargeback.ts`, `chargeback-agent.ts`, `chargeback-store.ts`).
